How to read this dashboard

This dashboard tracks what prediction markets say about when the Iran–Israel/US conflict will end. Prediction markets are platforms where people bet real money on whether future events will happen. Because participants have money on the line, prices tend to reflect genuine beliefs about probabilities — if a market is priced at 30%, that means traders collectively estimate a 30% chance the event will occur.

The data comes from Polymarket, the largest prediction market platform. Polymarket runs individual markets like "Will the conflict end by March 31?" — each priced between 0% and 100%. This dashboard combines all those markets into a single probability curve showing how likely the conflict is to end by any given date.

"End by date" (the default view) shows the running total: "What is the probability of the conflict ending by this date or earlier?" "End on date" shows the daily probability: "How likely is the conflict to end on this particular date?"

How the curve is built: Each Polymarket deadline market gives one data point (e.g. "30% chance by March 31"). The dashboard fits a smooth curve through these points using PCHIP interpolation (Piecewise Cubic Hermite Interpolating Polynomial), which preserves the monotonically increasing shape that a cumulative probability curve must have. The daily probability view is the derivative of this curve, computed via central differences.

Important: Prediction markets are not crystal balls. They reflect the crowd's best guess, which can be wrong. Markets can be swayed by low liquidity, misinformation, or sudden news events. Treat these numbers as one signal among many, not as ground truth. For a deeper explanation of how prediction markets work and their limitations, see Scott Alexander's Prediction Market FAQ.
